How the tape is built

fomopulse is a live trade tape: every buy and sell made by the tracked fomo.family wallets on Robinhood Chain, priced about a second after it lands. This page is what the numbers on it mean and where each one comes from, because a figure you cannot check is not worth reading.

There is no trade feed, only transfers

Robinhood Chain does not publish a list of trades. What it publishes is ERC-20 Transfer logs — one line saying some quantity of some token moved from one address to another. A swap is several of those inside one transaction, and turning them back into a trade is the whole of the work.

fomopulse subscribes to two filters, one for a tracked wallet as sender and one as recipient. When either fires the whole transaction is fetched, its transfers are read out, and the wallet's net flow of each token is worked out. A token that went up paired with cash that went down is a buy; the other way round is a sell.

What a fill is priced from, and what the tilde means

Most swaps carry their own price. The cash leg — USDG, or wrapped ether — is in the same transaction as the token leg, so the dollars are arithmetic and not an estimate. Those rows carry an exact figure.

Some do not. A router that nets several swaps together, or a trade paid for in something else, leaves a token leg with no cash beside it. Those are priced from the feed's quote for the token at that moment and marked with a ~. A fill that nothing could price at all carries a dash rather than a guess, and it is never quietly filled in later with today's price.

The dust rule

New tokens buy attention by sending themselves to wallets people watch. A thousand addresses receive a handful of a token nobody bought, and every tape that counts transfers shows a thousand trades that never happened.

A fill is kept off the tape on value and on shape together, never on size alone: a small buy is still a buy. What is filtered is an arrival with no cash paid, no history of the wallet trading the token, and the same arrival landing on many tracked wallets at once. If the token is later bought or sold for real, the earlier arrival is pardoned and reappears, because the thing that made it look like a handout has stopped being true. Handouts are counted and shown as a number on the token, never silently dropped.

Profit and loss is walked, not reported

Nothing numeric on these screens comes from anybody's API. The traders table is walked from this tape's own fills: average cost per wallet and token, a round trip counted in the window it closed in, unrealised marked at the feed's current price. Proceeds from selling something that arrived at no cost are shown apart, because that is not profit on anything.

fomo.family is asked for one thing and one thing only: who a trader is — the handle, the avatar, the clan. Every figure beside that name was measured here and can be checked against the chain, transaction by transaction.

Bags are positions, not balances

The bags screen is what the tracked wallets are still long, worked out from their own fills rather than read off a balance. What a wallet was given, or moved in from elsewhere, is not a position it took. Each row carries how many wallets are in it now against how many were when the window opened, so a bag being accumulated reads differently from one being left.

What a new token has to clear to be a discovery

The discover screen is pools opened in the last three days that a tracked wallet has bought into. Most young pools are not markets, so several cuts stand in front of the page:

  • A pool under ten thousand dollars deep is not a market and never appears.
  • A pool turning over more than twenty times its own depth in a day is not showing you the market's volume. That is the shape wash trading leaves behind.
  • A card the price feed has not answered for in an hour is dropped. A pool that rugs is not answered badly, it stops being answered — and the row keeps the depth, the volume and the change the pool had the hour it emptied, which reads as a find.
  • A pool the feed reports ten or more buys and not one sell from is dropped. The buy works for everyone and the sell works for nobody.
  • A token handed to more than five wallets for every real fill is dropped. The page ranks a pool on who is in it, and a launch that sprays the tracked wallets is buying that ranking.

What survives still carries what it costs to know: how long after the pool opened the first tracked wallet bought, what the whole token was worth at that moment against what it is worth now, and how many buys were cancelled by a sell of the same size minutes later.

Read-only, and open

fomopulse holds no keys, signs nothing and cannot trade. It never asks for a wallet, a seed phrase, a signature or a payment, and there is nothing on it to connect. It reads a public chain and shows you what it read. The same data is served as JSON at /api/tape, /api/traders, /api/bags and /api/discover, and every number the pages pace themselves by is at /api/limits.

Follow one of those links and you will read it. A program reading them on a schedule needs a key, sent as Authorization: Bearer, so that what it costs to answer can be counted against something rather than against everybody behind its address. Ask for one in an issue on the repository below; it is free, and the only reason it exists is that a name has to be checkable to mean anything.

It is not affiliated with fomo.family or with Robinhood Markets, and nothing here is advice about what to buy. The source is public: read the reconstruction yourself if a row looks wrong.
